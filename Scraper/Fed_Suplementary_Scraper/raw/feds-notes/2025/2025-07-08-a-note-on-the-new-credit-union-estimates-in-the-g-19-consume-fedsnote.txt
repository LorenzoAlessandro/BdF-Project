A Note on the New Credit Union Estimates in the G.19 "Consumer Credit" Statistical Release

Alexander Bruce, Michael M. Chernousov, Simona M. Hannon, and Marc J. Scott
*

Credit unions provide a significant shareâapproximately 14 percentâof consumer debt. As of 2025:Q1, consumer credit provided by credit unions was measured at over $716 billion in the 
G.19 Statistical Release
, "Consumer Credit," a Principal Federal Economic Indicator produced by the Federal Reserve Board. In this note, we discuss the construction and benchmarking of credit union estimates in the G.19, with a focus on a benchmark update together with a recent revision to the source data.

Credit Unions in the G.19 Statistical Release, "Consumer Credit"

The G.19 Statistical Release, "Consumer Credit," reports outstanding credit extended to individuals for household, family, and other personal expenditures, excluding loans secured by real estate by sector.
1
 Total consumer credit comprises two major types: revolving and nonrevolving (the latter sometimes being referred to also as installment credit). Revolving credit primarily consists of credit card debt, but it also includes other revolving credit plans linked to overdraft arrangements and other revolving credit plans not accessed by credit cards (Chernousov et al., 2024). Nonrevolving credit (that is, credit that is typically issued as a lump sum and is subject to a fixed repayment plan) consists of auto loans, student loans, and personal loans.

Credit unions provide both revolving and nonrevolving credit. Although these lenders only provide a small share of revolving credit, they play an important role in the provision of auto and personal loans, accounting for one-third of each of these market segments.
2

Construction of the G.19 Credit Union Estimates

In the G.19, we estimate monthly consumer credit for each major sector using lower-frequency benchmark and higher-frequency indicator data. Benchmark data are the most comprehensive and accurate measures of outstanding credit in each sector. These data may come from either administrative data sources or voluntary lender surveys.

For the credit union sector, benchmarking takes place quarterly.
3
 The G.19 monthly credit union estimates for revolving and nonrevolving credit are benchmarked to estimates constructed from the credit union Call Report data.
4
 Outstanding loans held by credit union Call Report filers are aggregated by component (that is, credit cards, auto loans, personal loans, and private student loans) and then by consumer credit type (that is, revolving and nonrevolving credit) to construct the benchmark estimates. Monthly indicator data complement the benchmark data in the construction of the G.19 estimates and are used to capture the monthly movements between the lower frequency benchmarks, as well as to extrapolate the movements in the months following the most recent benchmark.
5
 Specifically, to obtain the monthly estimates following the latest available benchmarking period, for each relevant credit category, we multiply the benchmark estimate by the growth rate of the corresponding monthly indicator series. The monthly estimates will subsequently be revised when the next quarterly benchmark arrives (see the 
About page
 of the release for further details on the use of benchmark and indicator data in the G.19).

Recent Updates to the Measurement of the Credit Union Sector

I. Benchmark Update

Starting with the publication of the May 2025 G.19 Statistical Release, "Consumer Credit," released on July 8, 2025, we implemented a benchmark formula update to capture the full spectrum of personal loans offered by credit unionsâunsecured and secured. The update consisted of the addition of secured personal loans held by credit unions to the quarterly benchmark of nonrevolving credit, as these loans were not previously included in the benchmark's construction. The new Call Report item (called "All Other Secured Non-Real Estate Loans/Lines of Credit") is marked with a star symbol in Table 1. The inclusion of this line item increases total outstanding credit held by credit unions reflected in the benchmark by approximately $69 billion but does not affect historical growth rates reflected on the G.19 Statistical Release, as a series break to the level series is included.
6

II. Indicator Series Data Source Update

Furthermore, starting with the December 2024 G.19 Statistical Release, "Consumer Credit" (released on February 7, 2025), we onboarded new source data for the credit union monthly indicator series. Before the December 2024 release, monthly indicator data were based on an industry survey and were provided by the Credit Union National Association (CUNA). However, the survey was discontinued. Starting with the December 2024 release, monthly indicator data are provided by America's Credit Unions (ACU) and are based on estimates derived from Analytic Dataset (ADS) Equifax credit bureau tradeline (or loan-level) data.
7
 Given the indicator series update, in what follows, we discuss how the new monthly indicator estimates compare with the benchmark, by credit typeârevolving and nonrevolvingâand component (or loan type).

1. Update of the Indicator Series for Revolving Credit

Revolving credit held by credit unions consists of credit card account balances as of the last day of the reporting period. Estimates of such credit are benchmarked to quarterly data on "Unsecured Credit Card Loans" reported in item 1 in the Loans and Leases section of the credit union Call Report. As of 2025:Q1, these balances stood at $84 billion. As shown in Figure 1, the monthly indicator Equifax-derived estimates provided by ACU closely follow the benchmark trend and stand at $88 billion. Over the entire time series horizon, the difference in between the benchmark and indicator is consistently small. (For a discussion on a variety of factors potentially contributing to the discrepancy, see Chernousov et al., 2024.)

2. Update of the Indicator Series for Nonrevolving Credit

Nonrevolving credit held by credit unions consists primarily of auto loans (1), both for new and used vehicles, and other nonrevolving credit loans (2), which comprise personal loansâsecured and unsecured, payday alternative loans (PAL), and non-federally guaranteed, or private, student loans.
8
 We discuss next how the monthly estimates compare to the Call Report benchmark for each major category of nonrevolving credit.

2.A. Auto Loans

The benchmark data for auto loans consist of the sum of outstanding balances for "New Vehicle Loans" and "Used Vehicle Loans" reflected in line items 5 and 6 in the Loans and Leases sector of the credit union Call Report. As of 2025:Q1, credit unions held about $322 billion in used and $164 billion in new vehicle loans, summing up to $486 billion in total outstanding auto loans. As shown in Figure 2, the monthly indicator estimates provided by ACU show a total of $479 billion as of March 2025. Historically, these estimates closely follow the benchmark trend but are slightly lower than the benchmark. The small difference between the benchmark Call Report data and the monthly indicator series derived based on credit bureau data might be attributed to delays in credit bureau reporting (Chernousov et al., 2024).

Conclusion

In this note, we discussed the construction of the new credit union estimates in the G.19. The adoption of new estimates was prompted by a benchmark update as well as a revision to our data source. We adopted the benchmark update starting with the May 2025 G.19, released on July 8, 2025. The new indicator estimates were implemented starting with the December 2024 G.19 Statistical Release, "Consumer Credit," which was released on February 7, 2025.
